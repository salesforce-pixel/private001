§ 02

Top current concerns

At-a-glance · what to watch and why
Capping tolerance — next 24–72 hoursWatch closely

The left drain was just capped, so bile now stays inside instead of draining to a bag. If it doesn’t clear well across the blockage, pressure and infection can build. Watch for fever or chills, deepening yellow in the eyes/skin, or belly pain — re-opening the drain is the rescue.

Biliary infection is held down, not clearedKeep antibiotics

The fever has settled for 2–3 days, but the white-cell count is rising and there are still bacteria in the bile — so the infection is being suppressed, not gone. Antibiotics (now with an antifungal added) should continue; the culture result is still awaited to fine-tune them.

Blood is clotting more slowly (INR 1.48)Needs vitamin K

The blood is taking longer to clot. It needs vitamin K and should be brought back toward normal before the chemo-port is placed, since that is a needle/line procedure with a bleeding risk.

Low sodium & mild acid build-upRecheck after capping

These came from blood taken just before the drain was capped, so they reflect the old leak. Capping should fix the cause — a repeat blood test is needed to confirm they recover. (Potassium, phosphate, calcium and magnesium have already normalised.)

Ongoing vomiting after foodSchedule anti-sickness meds

Vomiting is limiting how much she eats and which medicines she can keep down. Giving anti-sickness medicine on a fixed schedule before meals (rather than only when needed) should help, and correcting the low sodium will too.

Weakness & poor nutritionBuild strength for chemo

Weakness is the reason the next chemo cycle is delayed. Keeping up protein and calories — and adding nutrition support if she can’t eat enough — will help her be ready for cycle 2.
